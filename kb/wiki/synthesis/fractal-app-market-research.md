# Sell the flight, because depth is now free

almondbread should position itself as **the native Mac and iPad deep-zoom flight and video studio**: the one app where you fly through the Mandelbrot set at 120 Hz past 1e1000 and turn that flight into a shareable 4K video in one tap. It should not sell itself as "a deep-zoom Mandelbrot app". That label stopped being distinctive in 2026. At least six App Store apps launched or were rewritten between late 2025 and October 2026 claiming perturbation or arbitrary-precision depth. Several of them already bundle an autopilot dive, exact share links and video export. The closest copy of almondbread's plan, **Fractal Dive**, shipped on 2026-09-20 and costs $1.99 for Pro ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/fractal-dive/id6813485724)). Of the candidate differentiators, only one is unclaimed: **sustained real-time motion at extreme depth**. Every deep-zoom listing either "sharpens as you hold still" or makes no frame-rate claim. On the desktop, the serious deep-zoom tools are free, Windows-first offline renderers with no native Mac build. That leaves the Mac creator workflow (Kalles Fraktaler, then zoomasm or After Effects) empty. The market is small. Frax, the best-known iOS fractal app, has **232 US ratings after 13 years** ([iTunes lookup](https://itunes.apple.com/lookup?id=568827824&country=us)), so the business case is a niche premium product, not a mass-market app. The right model is a free download with full-depth exploration, plus a **$9.99 one-time Universal Purchase Pro unlock** gated on output (video, high-resolution stills, sync), and a later Mac "Studio" tier for creators. The launch should run through zoom videos and a technical Hacker News write-up rather than App Store search.

## Dozens of Apple fractal apps compete, and none has an audience

The Apple fractal category is long-tail and almost empty of traction. Only Fraksl passes 600 US ratings, and it is a video-feedback visualizer, not a Mandelbrot explorer. Frax, the incumbent with pedigree, has a **64-bit depth cap** that its own help page calls "absolute and final" ([Frax help](https://fr.ax/help/explore)). It updated in July 2026 after a 20-month gap with notes reading only "Bug fixes and improvements" ([iTunes lookup](https://itunes.apple.com/lookup?id=568827824&country=us)). The newcomers are what matter. **Mandelbrot Metal** released about 25 versions between February and October 2026 and moved to a subscription in v3.0: $2.99 a month, $19.99 a year or $39.99 lifetime ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/mandelbrot-metal/id6749807290)). Fractal Explorer: Deep Zoom, Ultra Mandelbrot, MandelKit, xFractal 2.0 and Unlimited Fractals all launched between July and October 2026, each with **0 to 28 US ratings**. Two conclusions follow. On Apple platforms, almondbread will launch into **five to ten look-alike "perturbation + dive + share link" apps**, all of them solo efforts with fast release cadences and none with an audience. The feature checklist cannot win there. Perceived quality, visible in a five-second preview, has to. Off Apple, the serious tools are free but hard and Windows-bound. Ultra Fractal is the only paid deep-zoom product, and **a deep-zoom video from it costs $99** ([Ultra Fractal shop](https://ultrafractal.com/shop)). Nobody sells polished deep-zoom software on the Mac, which is the opening. The steamroller risk is low: Frax's maintenance pattern shows no sign of a depth rewrite, and the free desktop tools have no Mac roadmap.

## Only real-time flight at depth is still unclaimed

Each candidate differentiator, measured against what is already shipping, falls into one of three groups. One is open, two are open only as a combination, and three are already parity features.

| Candidate differentiator | Who already ships it | Gap left | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| 120 Hz flight, game-like controls | Real-time only at shallow depth (Frax, XaoS, FractRoam). The only fps claim is "up to 60 fps" (Mandelbrot Flight Simulator). Deep apps are progressive. Only Fraksl advertises a gamepad. | Sustained motion at 1e300 to 1e1000+. Keyboard and gamepad flight on Mac. | **Primary differentiator**, if it is real and shown on video |
| Unlimited real-time depth (1e1000+) | Depth claims of 4e331 (Fractal Dive), 1e10000 (Ultra Mandelbrot), 1e20000 (Retinamandelbrot) | Depth while moving, not depth as such | Table stakes; sell it through flight |
| Autopilot to minibrots | Fractal Dive "Dive", FractRoam "Roam", Fractal Explorer "Fly", Retinamandelbrot autozoom | Autopilot that lands on a real minibrot at chosen depth, then hands over a video | Parity, plus a combination edge |
| One-tap zoom-video export | Fractal Dive Pro (1080p/4K), Frax Pro (1080p, criticised), Retinamandelbrot (gigabyte files), mandelbrot.site | High-bitrate 4K/8K with sane file sizes; Mac creator replacement for KF + zoomasm | **Second differentiator**, for the paying segment |
| Exact-coordinate links + iCloud | Frax, Fractal Dive, MandelKit (iCloud + links), Retinamandelbrot, mandelbrot.site | A link that previews on the web for people without the app | Parity; build it for virality, not for the pitch |
| Educational overlay (orbits, periods, Julia) | Live Julia in Mandelbrot Metal, Retinamandelbrot, Flight Simulator. Teaching chapters in Mysteries of Fractal. Free GeoGebra tools for classrooms. | Orbit and period overlay at depth | Nice to have; weak paid demand |

**Flight is the gap.** Fractal Explorer's own listing describes the state of the art: "the image sharpens as you hold still and keeps up while you move" ([lookup](https://itunes.apple.com/lookup?id=6794896393&country=us)). Mandelbrot Metal advertises "center-out refinement" ([lookup](https://itunes.apple.com/lookup?id=6749807290&country=us)). The demand evidence fits this gap. After depth, the most repeated complaint on Hacker News is "Zoom is jerky and the number of iterations don't automatically scale as I zoom in", and the answers point to decades-old XaoS and Fractint ([HN 43375676](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=43375676)). Frax reviewers attack its depth limit in every storefront year after year ("Please unlock infinity zoom !", DE 2023). They also attack its forced gyroscope ("Is there really no way to disable the gyroscope", US 2026) ([Frax reviews](https://itunes.apple.com/us/rss/customerreviews/page=1/id=568827824/sortby=mostrecent/json)). The engineering caveat is ours, not the market's. Our technical research shows float32 range runs out near 1e-30 and deep locations need millions of iterations. "120 Hz at 1e1000" is therefore only honest as 120 Hz *presentation* with reprojection and progressive refinement, not as full recomputation every frame ([[synthesis/deep-zoom-rendering-techniques]]). Marketing must show exactly what ships. One reviewer recording a stutter at 1e500 would undo the claim.

**Video is where the money is.** The creator pipeline is still Kalles Fraktaler on Windows exporting keyframes, assembled in zoomasm or in Maths Town's KF Movie Maker plug-in for After Effects. The plug-in's guide notes that "Adobe After Effects is not free" and that KF export can take "hours, days, weeks or months" ([KF Movie Maker guide](https://www.maths.town/after-effects-plugins/kfmoviemaker/kfmoviemaker-user-guide/)). Archived descriptions of Maths Town's "The Hardest Zoom" report over a month of 64-core CPU time for a ~7e1299 dive ([archive.org](https://archive.org/details/youtube-_zGNMiOBaPA)). zoomasm is "not tested" on macOS and writes ~21 GB of raw output per minute at 1080p60 ([zoomasm](https://mathr.co.uk/zoomasm/)). Some of the "weeks" figures are legacy, though. Current BLA renderers render a 5e-433 minibrot in about 30 seconds versus about 20 minutes in KF ([mathr KF page](https://mathr.co.uk/kf/kf.html)). Benchmark almondbread against today's tools, not against 2020 YouTube descriptions.

**Links, autopilot and education are hygiene.** Links matter because sharing already happens through URLs on HN ([HN 43375676](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=43375676)), not because anyone will buy the app for them. Autopilot answers a real complaint ("you'll end up in a flat space at some point when zooming", [HN 42586590](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=42586590)), and it doubles as a screensaver for the large relaxation audience. Demand for the educational overlay is real but diffuse. Teachers already have free GeoGebra material ([GeoGebra](https://www.geogebra.org/m/REQfacck)), so the overlay serves curious Numberphile and 3Blue1Brown viewers, not a classroom revenue line.

## Position as the native deep-zoom flight and video studio

The recommended positioning statement: **"Fly through the Mandelbrot set at 120 Hz, past 10^1000, and export the flight as a 4K video in one tap. Native on Mac, iPad and iPhone."** Three choices sit behind it. The lead verb is *fly*, not *zoom deeper*. Fractal Dive's copy ("Nearly every fractal app stalls around a thousand trillion times magnification", [lookup](https://itunes.apple.com/lookup?id=6813485724&country=us)) already occupies the depth framing, and "deeper than the others" is unprovable on a store page. Second, **Mac and iPad lead** because that is where the gap is structural. KF2+, Fraktaler 3, Imagina, FractalShark and Mandel Machine have no Mac build, and FractalShark's CUDA path is impossible on Apple Silicon ([FractalShark](https://github.com/mattsaccount364/FractalShark)). Third, the buyer is the **creator** who wants to make a Maths Town-style video without a Windows box. That segment is small but has the highest willingness to pay. The relaxation audience is huge but pays little. It is reached through autopilot and screensaver modes, and it fills the top of the funnel.

The audience numbers argue against mass-market ambitions. Deep-zoom videos have reached 4 to 12 million views each. Orson Wang's 2010 "10^275" zoom has 12.1M ([YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0jGaio87u3A)) and Maths Town's "The Hardest Trip" has 7.5M ([YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=LhOSM6uCWxk)). But the biggest are 6 to 16 years old, and **2026 long-form uploads get 10K to 60K views** ([YouTube, this year](https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=fractal+zoom&sp=CAMSAggF)). A third-party model puts Frax at 250k+ lifetime downloads ([MWM](https://mwm.ai/apps/fra/568827824)). With a typical 2 to 5% unlock rate, that implies lifetime IAP revenue on the order of **$25k to $125k**. That is our estimate with wide error bars, but it is the realistic ceiling to plan against.

The planned Windows/Linux port deserves scepticism. It would enter the home turf of free tools (KF, Fraktaler 3, Fractal Zoomer, FractalShark and a 2026 wave of Rust and CUDA hobby renderers). On Steam, fractal *tools* sit at 0 to 30 reviews, while the fractal *game* Fractal Block World has 1,652 ([Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1540570/)). A port pays off only if it is framed as an experience, and only after the Apple version proves the flight claim. RealMandelRender (curated destinations plus video, Windows, November 2026) and Fractaria (Mac + Windows studio, Q4 2026) show that others see the same "easy fractal" opening on Steam ([Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/5227800/); [Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/5157400/)).

## A minimum viable product built around flight and export

The MVP should include only what makes the positioning statement true, plus the hygiene features whose absence draws one-star reviews. The core is **flight**: 120 Hz presentation on ProMotion devices, with WASD/arrow keys, scroll, trackpad, touch and game controller, and **no forced gyroscope**. The engine is already decided in [[decisions/controls]]. Depth is unlimited to 1e1000+ with automatic iteration scaling and colouring that stays stable as iterations change, because HN users mistook low-iteration blobs for bugs ([HN 43375676](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=43375676)). A permanent depth HUD shows magnification, a+bi coordinates and a scale analogy. A Retinamandelbrot reviewer asked for coordinates ([GB reviews](https://itunes.apple.com/gb/rss/customerreviews/page=1/id=1509664268/sortby=mostrecent/json)), and Fractal Dive already uses "smaller than an atom" cues. **Autopilot** dives to a real minibrot or point of interest at a chosen depth and can be left running as an ambient mode. **One-tap video export** renders the last flight or autopilot path at 1080p, and at 4K for Pro, as HEVC at a bitrate that survives YouTube. Exports are vertical or horizontal and use sensible file sizes. Frax is criticised for pixelated and crashing recording, and Retinamandelbrot for gigabyte files ([Frax reviews](https://itunes.apple.com/us/rss/customerreviews/page=1/id=568827824/sortby=mostrecent/json); [Retinamandelbrot GB](https://itunes.apple.com/gb/rss/customerreviews/page=1/id=1509664268/sortby=mostrecent/json)). **Share links** carry exact coordinates and palette, open the app, and fall back to a web page with a preview image or clip and a store link. Bookmarks sync through iCloud. Palettes come with colour cycling ("that's been around for 30 years", [Fractally reviews](https://itunes.apple.com/us/rss/customerreviews/page=1/id=1534380451/sortby=mostrecent/json)). The educational layer is a light first cut: a live Julia preview for the point under the cursor, and an orbit trace on tap.

Defer the rest deliberately. Burning Ship, Celtic and other multibrot variants are the most-requested additions in reviews ([Retinamandelbrot US](https://itunes.apple.com/us/rss/customerreviews/page=1/id=1509664268/sortby=mostrecent/json); [Mandel Draw US](https://itunes.apple.com/us/rss/customerreviews/page=1/id=1547068084/sortby=mostrecent/json)) and are technically feasible ([[formulas/burning-ship]]). They make a good first update and a reason to return. Also defer the period and atom-domain overlay, music sync, EXR or zoomasm-compatible keyframe export, keyframed camera paths, 8K, an Apple TV or screensaver target, and visionOS. No search found demand for visionOS or VR. Ship nothing that is still flaky. The App Store punishes crashes and abandonment more than missing features. Retinamandelbrot keeps its "best Mandelbrot browser on iOS" reviews despite never being updated, but its crash reports pile up ([US reviews](https://itunes.apple.com/us/rss/customerreviews/page=1/id=1509664268/sortby=mostrecent/json)).

## One-time Universal Purchase, gated on output, no subscription

Charge for **output, not exploration**. The free tier gets full depth, full flight, autopilot, links and 1080p video with a small watermark. Pro, a **$9.99 one-time Universal Purchase** covering iPhone, iPad and Mac with Family Sharing, unlocks 4K video without a watermark, high-resolution stills, unlimited bookmarks with iCloud sync, all palettes and later formula packs. Gating depth would repeat Frax's most-hated limitation and lose to free deep apps. Gating links would kill virality, because the person opening a link has not bought anything yet. The value moment is the export button, so put the paywall there.

The price sits between the category's two anchors. The 2026 entrants charge $0.99 to $2.99 or give the app away, with Fractal Dive Pro at $1.99. Desktop pro tools charge $29 to $99 ([Ultra Fractal shop](https://ultrafractal.com/shop)), and Mandelbrot Metal's lifetime tier is $39.99 ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/mandelbrot-metal/id6749807290)). $9.99 signals premium quality without asking the relaxation audience for desktop money. Once flight, video and formulas have been proven, test $14.99. Add a **Mac "Studio" tier at about $29.99** later, aimed at creators: 8K, keyframed paths, music sync and EXR/zoomasm-compatible export. That follows the established category pattern of feature-gated one-time unlocks (GPU, animation, resolution) rather than content packs. Recurring revenue, if wanted, should come from paid major upgrades in the Ultra Fractal style.

Avoid a subscription. RevenueCat's 2026 data shows hard paywalls converting 10.7% versus 2.1% for freemium at day 35, and earning $3.09 versus $0.38 per install by day 60. But those are subscription apps with trials, and **about 72% of annual subscribers cancel in year one** ([RevenueCat](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/growth/subscription-app-trends-benchmarks-2026)). An offline explorer with no server costs gives little reason to renew. Most 2026 fractal launches advertise "no subscription". Mandelbrot Metal's switch is the experiment to watch: no review had reacted to it by mid-October ([US reviews](https://itunes.apple.com/us/rss/customerreviews/page=1/id=6749807290/sortby=mostrecent/json)). Accept that a free tier puts almondbread in the roughly 2% conversion regime, and make the free tier the marketing.

Distribute through the App Store under the Small Business Program (15%). The new EU terms, effective 2026-10-01, replace the Core Technology Fee with a 5% Core Technology Commission for distribution outside the App Store, and set link-out purchases at 10% for program members ([MacRumors](https://macrumors.com/2026/08/18/eu-app-store-fee-change)). For an indie already paying 15%, alternative distribution adds compliance work for close to no margin. Paddle direct sales of a Mac build net only about $1 more per $15 sale (5% + $0.50, [Paddle](https://www.paddle.com/compare/fastspring)) and lose Universal Purchase. Keep direct sales for later education site licences and a bundled Windows/Linux licence. A realistic first-year scenario, for planning only: 20,000 downloads × 3% × $9.99 × 0.85 ≈ **$5,000**. A **YouTube creator adopting the app**, or Apple editorial featuring, is the only thing that changes that order of magnitude.

## Launch through videos and a technical write-up, not search

What spreads in this niche is the **video artefact and the technical story**, not the app. Depth numbers in titles drive the all-time top zooms ("10^275", "7e1299"). Maths Town turned zoom videos into the self-described largest fractal art channel ([maths.town](https://www.maths.town/)), a Big Bang Theory licence ([maths.town](https://www.maths.town/videos/eye-of-the-universe-video/)) and NFTs. On Hacker News, technical deep dives reach 183 to 271 points ([HN 39683455](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=39683455); [HN 42586590](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=42586590)), while bare Mandelbrot viewers mostly get 2 to 5 points. mandelbrot.site scored 5 points on its own Show HN and 86 when someone else reposted it ([HN 41720454](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=41720454); [HN 43375676](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=43375676)). App Store search volume for "fractal" is unknown and almost certainly low, so ASO is hygiene, not a channel.

**Pre-launch, 6 to 8 weeks.** Render three to five depth-titled flights in almondbread (for example "Real-time flight to 10^1000, recorded on an M1") and post them on YouTube and as vertical Shorts and TikTok clips. Put the app name in the description and a subtle watermark, so the recurring "what software?" question answers itself. Stand up the web landing page and the share-link resolver first, because every clip and link should land there. Run a TestFlight beta seeded from fractalforums.org and r/fractals. Approach two or three creators (Maths Town, SeryZone, Fractal Adventures) with early builds and an offer of high-bitrate export. Creator influence on software choice is plausible but unverified, so treat it as a bet. Record the App Preview video as continuous flight at extreme depth with the depth HUD visible. That single clip is the differentiator, and no competitor's listing can show it.

**Launch week.** Release on all three Apple platforms at once. On the same day, publish a technical write-up on the blog and submit it to HN: real-time perturbation at 1e1000 on a float32-only Apple GPU, with reproducible benchmarks against current BLA renderers, not against 2020 KF numbers. Pitch Apple editorial through the featuring channel. The 2026 Apple Design Award finalists in "Delight and Fun" included the small generative toy Metaballs ([9to5Mac](https://9to5mac.com/2026/05/18/apple-unveils-30-apple-design-award-app-finalists/)), which makes a Metal-native, design-idiomatic explorer a credible featuring candidate. Featuring cannot be planned, but it is the largest single lever. A launch price of $6.99 for Pro in the first week gives coverage a hook.

**After launch.** Ship Burning Ship and its relatives as the first update. Post a weekly short clip found by autopilot. Run a "deepest minibrot" challenge using share links. Add the screensaver and Apple TV mode for the relaxation audience. Build the Studio tier once creators are using export. Revisit Windows/Linux only if creators ask for it, and then launch on Steam as an experience with a 5 to 10k wishlist target. GameDiscoverCo's median first-week sales of 0.10 to 0.20× wishlists put that at roughly 1 to 2k sales ([GameDiscoverCo](https://newsletter.gamediscover.co/p/steam-the-new-wishlists-to-first)). Speed matters more than polish on secondary features. New deep-zoom apps arrived at a rate of several a month in Q3 2026, and the first one to show credible flight at depth in a preview video will own the description.

## Conclusion

The market research changes the project's thesis. In the plan, depth, autopilot, links and video were four differentiators. In October 2026 they are four checkboxes that a $1.99 app shipped three weeks ago. What remains defensible is an engineering claim, smooth motion at extreme depth, and a workflow claim: a Mac replacement for a Windows-and-After-Effects video pipeline. Both rest on the renderer roadmap, not on features. Work that makes flight at 1e1000 genuinely smooth (frame reuse, LA skip tables, chunked scheduling) is now product strategy as much as engineering. Anything that delays a convincing 30-second flight video costs more than any missing feature.

The money will stay modest whatever the positioning. The whole Apple category has a few thousand ratings in total, and the incumbent's lifetime revenue is likely in five or low six figures. almondbread is best treated as a craft product whose upside depends on a few non-linear events: Apple featuring, a creator adopting it, or a viral clip.
