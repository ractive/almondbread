# IAU astronomical unit and the light-year

Not saved under `raw/`. Read 2026-10-15.

## Astronomical unit

- **Value:** 149 597 870 700 m, exact.
- **Quote:** "Resolution B2 of the XXVIII General Assembly of the IAU in 2012 defined the
  astronomical unit to be a conventional unit of length equal to 149 597 870 700 m
  exactly." Taken from IAU 2015 Resolution B3 (nominal solar and planetary conversion
  constants), endnote 4:
  <https://www.iau.org/common/Uploaded%20files/IAUGA2015-Resolution-B3-recommended-nominal-conversion.pdf>.
- The 2012 resolution PDF itself (`iau.org/static/resolutions/IAU2012_English.pdf`)
  returned 404 on 2026-10-15; the B3 text is an IAU primary document restating it.
- Reliability: high (an IAU resolution defines the unit).

## Light-year

- **Value:** 9 460 730 472 580 800 m, exact by construction: the distance light travels
  in vacuum in one Julian year, `c × 365.25 × 86 400 s` with `c = 299 792 458 m/s`
  exact (see [[sources/codata-2022-constants]]). Arithmetic checked 2026-10-15.
- **Quotes found:**
  - NIST Guide to the SI (SP 811), Appendix B.8: "light year (l.y.) [18] meter (m)
    9.460 73 E+15" (rounded to 6 figures):
    <https://www.nist.gov/pml/special-publication-811/nist-guide-si-appendix-b-conversion-factors/nist-guide-si-appendix-b8>.
    The footnote [18] that names the year length was not reachable.
  - IAU Office of Astronomy for Education glossary: "It is defined as the distance that
    light travels in vacuum in one year: 9.46 trillion kilometers (km)":
    <https://astro4edu.org/resources/glossary/term/178/>.
- I found no live IAU page that prints all 16 digits (the old IAU "measuring the
  universe" and style-manual unit pages return 404). The Julian-year definition (IAU 1976
  system of constants) is stated on secondary pages only. The exact 16-digit figure is
  therefore (unverified against an IAU page) but follows from two exact definitions.
- Reliability: the value is not in doubt; only the IAU wording is secondary.

## Also in NIST SP 811 B.8

"astronomical unit (au) meter (m) 1.495 979 E+11", consistent with the above.
