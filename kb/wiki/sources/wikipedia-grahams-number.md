## What it is

Encyclopedia article on Graham's number. Read through a summarising fetch on 2026-10-15.

## What it covers

- `g₁ = 3↑↑↑↑3`, `g_n = 3 ↑^(g_(n-1)) 3`, `G = g₆₄`: "the number of arrows in each layer is
  specified by the value of the next layer below it".
- Martin Gardner popularised it in Scientific American's "Mathematical Games", November 1977,
  from an unpublished bound by Graham; Graham and Rothschild's 1971 paper ("Ramsey's Theorem for
  n-Parameter Sets", Trans. AMS) has a different explicit bound.
- Numbers far larger have since appeared in serious proofs, e.g. TREE(3) (Friedman's finite form
  of Kruskal's theorem).
- No comparison with Moser's number on this page (see [Wikipedia: Steinhaus–Moser notation (mega, Moser's number)](wikipedia-steinhaus-moser-notation.md)).
