## Seahorse valley
place: seahorse-valley
interior: flat
orbit: off
map: off
marker: off

The big heart-shaped part of the set is called the **heart**. On its left, it touches a big round part at one point, the **neck**. In this view, magnified 87 times, the black area on the right is the heart, and the black area on the left is the big round part. The neck is below this view. The gap between them, just above the neck, is **seahorse valley**.

Both walls of the valley are lined with curled shapes that people call seahorses. They are made of small **bulbs**, round parts like the black circles along the walls, on long chains. Every chain ends in a **spiral**.

## A seahorse
place: seahorse

One seahorse, close up. The big black circle on the right is a bulb on the edge of the heart. From it, a curled body of tiny bulbs and branches reaches to the left. It ends in a tail that winds into a dense spiral, below and to the left of the middle of this view.

Parts of the next seahorses are at the top and at the bottom of this view. All the seahorses in the valley have this shape. The further down the valley, towards the neck, the smaller they are.

## The tail
place: seahorse-tail

A seahorse tail, close up. It spirals in, turn after turn, towards one point a little to the right of and below the middle of this view. This point is a **Misiurewicz point** (named after the mathematician Michał Misiurewicz).

Remember the formula: start with z = 0 and use z → z² + c again and again. For most points c, z either escapes or comes closer and closer to a loop of a few places. At a Misiurewicz point it does neither. After some steps z lands **exactly** on a **loop**: a few values that repeat always. But if z is a little away from the loop, the next steps push it further away.

## A double spiral
place: two-arm-spiral
marker: -0.775683768009053797469483503934741045728, 0.136467368294690124733274409617848765198
marker-label: M(24,2)

Another spiral in the valley. Its centre, the gold circle labelled M(24,2), is another Misiurewicz point. Its z lands after **24 steps** on a loop of **2** values, so it is called **M(24,2)**.

The two arms of the spiral wind into the centre. Each turn is a little smaller than the one before.

## The same, 10⁵⁰ times deeper
place: spiral-1e50
marker: off

This is the same point, magnified **10⁵⁰** times (a 1 with 50 zeros) instead of about 30 000 times. It looks the same: two arms that still turn.

Near a Misiurewicz point the set copies itself: zoom in by a certain amount, turn by a certain angle, and you see the same picture again. That is why spirals never end, however far you zoom in.

## Elephant valley
place: elephant-valley
move: jump

On the right side of the heart there is a dent, the **cusp**. In this view the heart is the black area, and the cusp is the sharp point in the middle, on the left. The gap that opens to the right of the cusp is **elephant valley**. Its walls are lined with shapes like elephants, with trunks that curl inwards.

## The herd
place: elephant-herd

Elephants in a row along the edge of the heart, the black area at the bottom left. They get smaller towards the top left, where the cusp is. Each elephant grows out of a small bulb on the heart (the black circles along the edge), and each trunk curls into a spiral around a Misiurewicz point.

## Branch points
place: three-way-junction

Not every Misiurewicz point is the centre of a spiral. This one, in the middle of the view, is a **fork**: three branches meet here. They are branches of the **antenna**, the thin line on top of the bulb above the heart. After 4 steps, the z of this point lands on one value and stays there.

## The same fork, a googol deeper
place: junction-1e100

The same point, magnified **10¹⁰⁰** times (a *googol*: a 1 with 100 zeros). It is still a fork with three branches, now covered in star shapes with many arms. At every depth, the set is built around these points: spirals and forks.
