# Autopilot, journeys & places

## Driving levels

Like a car, Almondbread has four ways to drive, shown at the bottom of the picture as **M · C · A · J**: **M**anual, **C**ruise, **A**utopilot and **J**ourney. Next to them a thin gauge shows the speed: zooming out to the left, zooming in to the right, with the value, for example *↓ ×2/s* (out, twice as wide every second) or *↑ ×1.5/s* (in).

## Cruise

**E** and **Q** are pedals. From rest, **E** starts a steady zoom in and **Q** a zoom out; each press is one step faster, and holding the key keeps speeding up. The other pedal slows down step by step and stops at zero: to go the other way, let go and press again. **Esc** brakes gently to a stop.

You always steer yourself: point with the mouse (or a finger on iPhone and iPad) where you want to go, or use **W A S D**. Nothing pulls you anywhere.

Zooming out gets faster the deeper you are, so the way home never takes long: about a minute from the deepest views at the first step. The speed builds up over a few seconds, and near the whole set it slows down evenly and comes to rest exactly on it.

**Lane assist** (**L**, or the control center) keeps a cruise out of featureless areas with a gentle nudge; your own steering always wins. The lane icon in the gauge lights up while it is on.

## Autopilot

Press **Z** (or the plane button) and the view keeps zooming on its own, heading for interesting places: spirals, branch points and tiny copies of the whole set.

- **E** and **Q**, the mouse wheel or a two-finger scroll change the speed.
- **Click** (or tap) pauses and resumes.
- Steer yourself (move the mouse, **W A S D**, drag) and you take the wheel: the view cruises on at the same speed. The autopilot does not take over again by itself; press **Z** for that. **Q** down to zero also hands over to cruise, which then stops.
- **Esc** stops.

When the picture ahead gets plain, or the computer needs time to keep up, the autopilot slows down by itself; the status overlay says why.

## Journeys

Press **J** (or the sparkles button) for a journey: an endless flight from landmark to landmark. Almondbread finds the landmarks in the maths itself: tiny copies of the whole set, spirals, and the patterns on the way to them. Each leg flies to one, rests a moment so the view can sharpen, and flies on.

- Steering yourself, or **Q** down to zero, takes the wheel: the view cruises on (or stops) and the journey pauses. Other input pauses it too; **J** or a click resumes. **E** and **Q** change its speed.
- **Esc** stops.
- **Stop at each find** (control center, Journey): the journey waits at each landmark, so the picture can finish. The status overlay names it, for example *Minibrot, period 34*. Then **J** flies on as planned, **Z** goes deeper here, **X** goes somewhere else, and **Esc** ends the journey. Look around in the meantime as you like; if you wander far off, the journey continues from where you are. On iPhone and iPad, use the buttons next to the journey button.
- **J** starts a journey in the mode set in the control center, **Shift + J** in the other one.
- Every journey has a number, its seed. The same seed from the same start gives the same journey, so you can share it. The **daily journey** is the same for everyone on the same day.

## Places

The control center starts with **Places**: famous views, from the seahorse valley to views deeper than a googol. **Go** jumps there, **Fly** flies there, **Random** picks one for you.

## Fly to coordinates

Every view has an address: its centre and its zoom. **⌘C** copies it, **⌘V** jumps to a copied one (also **⇧⌘C** / **⇧⌘V** while a text field has focus), **⇧⌘G** flies to it, and **⌘G** flies to an address you type.
